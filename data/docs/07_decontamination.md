# Decontamination Procedures

Decontamination is the response to a spill, contamination event, or
suspected biohazard exposure. The procedure depends on the agent and the
affected surface, but the overall flow is consistent.

## Immediate Containment

The first priority is to contain the affected area without spreading
contamination. If the spill is liquid, place absorbent material around
its perimeter to halt spread. Do not begin wiping inward yet. If the
spill is on or near a device, power down the device only if it can be
done safely without leaning over the affected area.

## Personal Protective Equipment

Put on a fresh pair of gloves and a lab coat before approaching the
spill. For volatile or biological agents, add a face shield and a fluid-
resistant apron. Discard PPE into the dedicated waste stream after the
procedure — do not reuse.

## Cleaning Agent Selection

Choose the cleaning agent based on the contaminating substance:

- Biological material: a freshly prepared 1 percent sodium hypochlorite
  solution, applied for a minimum contact time of ten minutes.
- Most organic chemicals: a 70 percent isopropyl alcohol solution.
- Acid or base spills: neutralize with the appropriate spill kit before
  any further cleaning.

When in doubt, consult the Safety Data Sheet for the contaminating
substance. The Lab Operations Hub indexes every SDS used in the
building.

## Sequence

Work from the outside of the contaminated area inward, using
single-direction wipes. Discard each wipe after a single pass. Repeat
with fresh wipes until the area is visibly clean, then apply the
chosen agent a final time and respect the indicated contact time.

## Recording the Event

Every decontamination event is recorded in the Lab Operations Hub
incident log. Capture what was spilled, where, the volume, the
cleaning agent used, the contact time, and any disposal that resulted.
Records are required for compliance audits.

## When to Open a Separate Incident

Open a ServiceNow incident in addition to the Hub record when a device
needs servicing as a result of the contamination, or when personal
exposure may have occurred.
